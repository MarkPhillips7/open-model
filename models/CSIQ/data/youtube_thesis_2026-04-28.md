## Operating points that changed the plan rows

- Storage is the profitable growth engine. He cited ~7.8 GWh and about $1.4B of storage revenue for 2025, a $3.6B backlog, and an expectation of 14–17 GWh in 2026. Q2 2026's release did not reiterate 14–17 GWh. It reiterated 4.5–5.5 GWh and 6.5–7.0 GW of modules for the **US** market. The yellow 2026 plans follow the reiterated guide. The 14–17 GWh figure is not in the default path.
- HJT is his long-term US technology bet because the US has no legacy PERC/TOPCon base to upgrade cheaply. TOPCon wins near term on cost. He says being wrong on HJT does not break the base case.
- Recurrent was bought years ago (the clip he plays is of the old Recurrent acquisition, about $360M) so the manufacturing business could see the end market. The company was, in that older clip, shifting from pure development toward owning and operating. Q1 2026 comments from Recurrent's then-CEO match that: sell some assets to delever, keep a fleet.
- BlackRock's 20% is the cleanest third-party equity mark in the story. It is from early 2024. The achieved lever is where age and the soft 2026 quarter belong.
- He argues the US factories are the mispriced piece, because a sell-side model can zero them and still talk about CSI Solar and Recurrent.
